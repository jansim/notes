### title

5 Years of Citizen Science - Lessons Learned and Software Built

### abstract

In early 2019 I visited Samuel Mehr as an intern and helped to quietly launch themusiclab.org, a citizen science platform where people can take part in gamified experiments, typically related to the perception of music. In the 5 1/2 years that followed, the platform has attracted more than 5,000,000 players from across the world and provided data for multiple publications. To get it there, we had to navigate surprise (and sometimes overnight) peaks in traffic, technical difficulties, bugs and the integration of various studies over time.
We used our learnings across the past few years to inform the development of a new software backend which has been powering the site over the last year. The software, titled World-Wide-Lab is available as free and open-source software, developed specifically to easily collect online citizen science data, with a special eye on enabling the gamification of studies.
The colloquium will start with a look at some of the lessons we learned while running themusiclab.org over the past few years before transitioning to an interactive workshop, where everyone will get the chance to build and run their own gamified mini-study and collect data.
### bio

Jan Simson is a doctoral candidate in Statistics at the LMU Munich, the Munich Center for Machine Learning and the Konrad Zuse School of Excellence in Reliable AI. He has been working for and collaborating with The Music Lab for the past few years, developing and maintaining the citizen science platform themusiclab.org. Jan holds a degree in Psychology: Behavioural Data Science from the University of Amsterdam as well as a vocational degree as Specialized Computer Scientist in Software Development. He has worked as a developer, data analyst and researcher across both industry and research. His research focuses on improving practices for the collection and usage of data as well as making machine learning more fair.
